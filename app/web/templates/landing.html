01 · how it routes

Four difficulty bands. Three tiers. Every start is measured.

Each prompt is matched against 176 labelled questions with sentence embeddings, which places it in a band. Each tier has a calibrated score on every band, and a band starts at the cheapest tier that clears 80%. This table is the router's live configuration, not an illustration.

Why a learned scorer and an LLM judge

FrugalGPT replaces the LLM judge with a small learned model. We built that, then measured it against the judge on the same answers: the judge catches 94% of wrong cheap answers, the scorer about half. So the scorer never replaces the judge; it decides when the judge is needed. Its accept threshold sits at the highest band where zero wrong answers slipped through held-out testing (0 of 15 at P ≥ 0.95).

What that saves depends on what a verdict costs relative to the answer it protects. Here the judge is 2% of a mid-tier answer, so the gate trims a few percent. With the judge on the tier above, where a verdict costs about what the next answer would, the same code decides whether a cascade pays for itself at all.

03 · what it saved

41% under the mid tier, and it took two stacks to get there.

The first stack tied its own mid-tier model exactly: the judge was the mid model, so verification cost what it saved. Two changes turned the tie into a saving: a cheap tier that clears the bar on more questions, and a judge 50× cheaper than a mid answer.

04 · bring your own models

Your providers, your keys, a routing map derived from your numbers.

Connect a provider, list its models, calibrate each on 24 graded questions. Those scores are the routing map. Change the models and it re-derives itself.

Calibrate once

Scores belong to the model, not the slot. Use it as cheap in one session and mid in another.

Keys stay yours

A provider key lives in your browser for the session, or encrypted at rest if you opt in.

Pinned per session

A chat keeps the models it was created with, so its history stays coherent.

OpenAI-shaped

One endpoint. A normal completion back, plus where it routed and what it saved.

# route a request through your own models
curl -X POST https://your-host/api/v1/route \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer rtr_your_key" \
  -d '{
    "messages": [{"role": "user", "content": "What is 2+2?"}],
    "models": {"cheap": "groq/openai/gpt-oss-20b", "frontier": "openai/gpt-5"}
  }'
